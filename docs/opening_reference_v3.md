# Opening revision: footage references

Only the opening (at most ten seconds) is being revised. Existing remaining footage and audio are retained.

## Primary footage inspected

1. USGS/Hawaiian Volcano Observatory, “Fifth explosive eruption,” August 27, 2008. Public domain; 26 seconds. https://www.usgs.gov/media/videos/fifth-explosive-eruption
   Downloaded reference: `references/usgs_2008.mp4`. Displayed source label indicates ×7 speed. Sequential frames show white degassing, followed by denser gray ash that develops new irregular lobes and overtakes earlier emissions.
2. USGS/Hawaiian Volcano Observatory, “Explosive eruption from Halema‘uma‘u,” October 12, 2008. Public domain; 39 seconds, ×3 speed. https://www.usgs.gov/media/videos/explosive-eruption-halemaumau
   Downloaded reference: `references/usgs_oct2008.mp4`. Sequential frames show a rapidly expanding ash-rich pulse and continued vent supply. Older lobes and the lower stem evolve differently rather than enlarging as one rigid silhouette.

These smaller Kīlauea events are motion and appearance references, not equivalents of Tambora's magnitude, topography, or eruption chronology. Their indicated playback speeds must not be interpreted as real-time motion. Any use in compositing remains a reconstructed Tambora scene, not historical Tambora footage.

## Changes sought

- Remove the miniature, overly sharp, uniformly enlarging mushroom silhouette in the first five seconds.
- Make new emissions originate at a fixed vent while earlier material rises and drifts.
- Match atmospheric haze and distant contrast to the landscape.
- Maintain moving sea, fixed terrain, continuous exposure, and 24 fps output.
- Review the actual encoded opening and its join to the preserved remainder.

## Delivered scope

Only the corrected first five seconds are delivered as an approval sample. The attempted 5–8-second transition was rejected for visible ghosting and is not part of the deliverable. Existing complete v2 film is unchanged. First-five-second plume uses extracted moving imagery from the August 27 USGS film, at fixed image scale and about 5.7× real-time speed, composited at the reconstructed summit.
